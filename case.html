Bring your USCIS case information

You stay in control. N-400 Journey does not ask for your USCIS password or log into your account.

🔒 Your case information stays on this device.
  • We don't need your USCIS password.
  • Your pasted case data is processed in your browser.
  • Your case information is not uploaded to a server.
  • You don't need to create an account.
How privacy works

Outbound connections are blocked by this page's Content-Security-Policy, which your browser enforces whether the code behaves or not (view source to verify) — this page is not just promising not to send data, it's technically unable to. A small status summary (not the full record) is saved on this device so it can show you what's changed since your last check; nothing is ever transmitted, and "Clear saved status" removes it at any time.

📖 How to bring your case information

You get a copy of your own case information directly from USCIS — it travels straight from USCIS to your screen, and this page is never in the middle.

1Sign in to your USCIS account — open myaccount.uscis.gov and log in the way you normally would.
2Open your case information — in a new tab of that same browser, visit
https://my.uscis.gov/account/case-service/api/cases/<your-receipt-number>
Replace <your-receipt-number> with your own receipt number (the IOE… code printed on your USCIS notices) at the end.
3Copy the case data — you'll see a wall of text beginning with {"data":. Select it all (Ctrl+A, or Cmd+A on a Mac) and copy it (Ctrl+C / Cmd+C).
4Return here and paste it — paste into the box below and press Analyze My Case.

The information is formatted as JSON behind the scenes — you don't need to understand it, just copy and paste it. Seeing an error page instead of text at that URL? Your USCIS session expired — sign in again, then reload the tab. The whole trip happens between your browser and USCIS: this site has no servers, cannot reach USCIS on your behalf, and never sees your login.

🗽 About N-400 Journey

          
        
        

          
N-400 Journey is a personal project by one person
            (rohitbobbili),
            not a company — a free companion for people navigating their own N-400 naturalization case. It has
            two independent tools.


          
Case Journey reformats the case JSON your own USCIS account already gives you into
            a plain-language event timeline. It can't look anything up or predict a decision — it only
            reorganizes data you already have. Event-code names follow the publicly published
            NIEM
            v5.0 code list; the plain-language explanations are my own reading of those codes, not official
            USCIS definitions.


          
Learning Journey is a practice quiz for the 2025 civics test's 128 official
            questions, sourced from the
            official
            USCIS 2025 Civics Test PDF, including a Test Simulation mode built to the real interview format
            (up to 20 questions, 12 correct required).


          
Both run entirely in your browser. There are no servers, no accounts, and no
            analytics — case data and practice progress never leave your device. That isn't only a promise: the
            page's Content-Security-Policy forbids outbound connections and form submissions, and your browser
            enforces that whether the code behaves or not (view source to verify). Case Journey keeps a small
            status summary in local storage — status, key dates, event codes seen — so it can tell you what's
            changed the next time you check; it never keeps the full record you pasted, and "Clear saved
            status" removes it.


          
The entire source is public under an MIT licence at
            github.com/rohitbobbili/uscis-tracker.
            If something looks wrong, please
            open
            an issue — that's the fastest way to reach me.
