📖 How to Get Your Case JSON

The tracker reads a copy of your case record that you fetch yourself — it travels straight from USCIS to your screen, and this page is never in the middle.

1Sign in to USCIS — open myaccount.uscis.gov and log in the way you normally would.
2Open your case's data URL — in a new tab of that same browser, visit
https://my.uscis.gov/account/case-service/api/cases/<your-receipt-number>
Replace <your-receipt-number> with your own receipt number (the IOE… code printed on your USCIS notices) at the end.
3Copy everything on that page — you'll see a wall of text beginning with {"data":. Select it all (Ctrl+A, or Cmd+A on a Mac) and copy it (Ctrl+C / Cmd+C).
4Come back and analyze — paste into the box at the top of this page and press Analyze Case.

Seeing an error page instead of text at that URL? Your USCIS session expired — sign in again, then reload the tab. The whole trip happens between your browser and USCIS: this site has no servers, cannot reach USCIS on your behalf, and never sees your login.

🗽 About N-400 Journey

N-400 Journey is a personal project by one person (rohitbobbili), not a company — a free companion for people navigating their own N-400 naturalization case. It has two independent tools.

Case Journey reformats the case JSON your own USCIS account already gives you into a plain-language event timeline. It can't look anything up or predict a decision — it only reorganizes data you already have. Event-code names follow the publicly published NIEM v5.0 code list; the plain-language explanations are my own reading of those codes, not official USCIS definitions.

Learning Journey is a practice quiz for the 2025 civics test's 128 official questions, sourced from the official USCIS 2025 Civics Test PDF, including a Test Simulation mode built to the real interview format (up to 20 questions, 12 correct required).

Both run entirely in your browser. There are no servers, no accounts, and no analytics — case data and practice progress never leave your device. That isn't only a promise: the page's Content-Security-Policy forbids outbound connections and form submissions, and your browser enforces that whether the code behaves or not (view source to verify).

The entire source is public under an MIT licence at github.com/rohitbobbili/uscis-tracker. If something looks wrong, please open an issue — that's the fastest way to reach me.
