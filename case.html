📖 How to Get Your Case JSON

The tracker reads a copy of your case record that you fetch yourself — it travels straight from USCIS to your screen, and this page is never in the middle.

1Sign in to USCIS — open myaccount.uscis.gov and log in the way you normally would.
2Open your case's data URL — in a new tab of that same browser, visit
https://my.uscis.gov/account/case-service/api/cases/<your-receipt-number>
Replace <your-receipt-number> with your own receipt number (the IOE… code printed on your USCIS notices) at the end.
3Copy everything on that page — you'll see a wall of text beginning with {"data":. Select it all (Ctrl+A, or Cmd+A on a Mac) and copy it (Ctrl+C / Cmd+C).
4Come back and analyze — paste into the box at the top of this page and press Analyze Case.

Seeing an error page instead of text at that URL? Your USCIS session expired — sign in again, then reload the tab. The whole trip happens between your browser and USCIS: this site has no servers, cannot reach USCIS on your behalf, and never sees your login.

🗽 About This Tool

I built this while trying to make sense of my own USCIS case record — the data your account returns is accurate but unreadable, and I wanted to see it as a timeline instead of a wall of JSON. It is a personal project maintained by one person (rohitbobbili), not a company, and there is no organisation behind it.

What I am not: I am not an immigration attorney, a DOJ-accredited representative, or a USCIS employee, and nothing here is legal advice. This page does one narrow thing — it reformats data you already have. It cannot look anything up, predict a decision, or tell you what to do about your case. For that, talk to someone qualified.

Where the information comes from: event-code names follow the publicly published NIEM v5.0 code list; the plain-language explanations are my own reading of those codes and are not official USCIS definitions. Your case data comes from your own account at my.uscis.gov. The authority on your case is always USCIS itself — if this page and an official notice disagree, the notice is right.

How to check my work, or tell me I got it wrong: the entire source is public under an MIT licence at github.com/rohitbobbili/uscis-tracker, so you can read exactly what it does rather than trust a claim on a page. If an event code is explained wrongly or something looks broken, please open an issue — that is the fastest way to reach me, and corrections are welcome.

Last reviewed 26 September 2026. Event-code coverage and wording are checked against the NIEM list when USCIS changes it; the date above is when that was last done.
